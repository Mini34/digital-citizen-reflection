2 · Evaluate the evidence

Read the evidence relevant to your topic. Compare a finding with its limits: a source can be credible without answering every question.

WHO Regional Office for Europe

Online habits and well-being

Date: 2024-09-25

The survey found signs of problematic social media use in 11% of respondents. Heavy use without those problems was also associated with stronger peer support.

Who it covers & what it cannot tell us

Population / audience: Almost 280,000 young people aged 11, 13 and 15 in 44 countries and regions in Europe, central Asia and Canada; surveyed in 2022.

Limit: A population survey does not diagnose an individual or establish a universal safe number of minutes. Associations do not prove that one habit caused an outcome.

What is the habit helping you do, and what is it getting in the way of?

UNESCO

Judging information before using it

Date: Publication date not stated; checked 2026-10-04

UNESCO describes media literacy as the skills needed to engage critically with information and navigate digital environments safely.

Who it covers & what it cannot tell us

Population / audience: General media and information literacy guidance for people engaging with information online.

Limit: This is educational guidance, not an experiment measuring how much a particular checking routine improves accuracy.

Who made the claim, what original evidence supports it, and what context is missing?

Read the original source

Government of Canada · Get Cyber Safe

Keeping personal information private

Date: Page modified 2021-10-27; checked 2026-10-04

Get Cyber Safe recommends unique account passwords and multi-factor authentication, and asks users to take precautions before posting on social media.

Who it covers & what it cannot tell us

Population / audience: General guidance for people using online accounts; not a participant study.

Limit: These steps reduce particular exposures. They cannot guarantee account security or remove information that others have already copied.

Could a post or prompt reveal more about you or someone else than you intended?

Read the original source

UNESCO · Fengchun Miao and Wayne Holmes

Human judgment when using generative AI

Date: Published 2023-09-07; overview updated 2026-01-16

UNESCO's guidance emphasizes human agency, data privacy, and educational validation when using generative AI.

Who it covers & what it cannot tell us

Population / audience: Global guidance for education and research, including educators and policy-makers.

Limit: Policy guidance is not a test of a specific AI tool. A polished answer still needs evidence appropriate to the task.

Four ways to begin

All examples are fictional.

Screen time & attention

Look at when and why you use a screen, and what you want that time to do.

Fictional observation: I plan to scroll for ten minutes, then keep going for about an hour.

Social media & privacy

Consider the audience for a post and the details it reveals.

Fictional observation: I share photos from a club meeting before checking what is in the background.

News & information

Trace an appealing claim to its source before sharing it.

Fictional observation: I almost share a viral post saying our city's buses will be free next month.

AI-assisted tools

Decide what the tool can help with and what you need to check yourself.

Fictional observation: I use an AI summary as a starting point without checking its references.
